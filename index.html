Research problem

Complex engineering systems fail through mechanisms, not diagrams.

My work asks how interdependent infrastructure can be represented without losing the physics, causality, time scales, uncertainty and operational constraints that make the system real.

01 · REALITY

What actually exists?

Components, flows, capacities, services, hazards, spatial structure, operating limits and measurable states define the engineering system before symbols do.

02 · CAUSALITY

How does one subsystem change another?

Dependencies require a defensible mechanism: source, receiver, direction, magnitude, delay, activation condition, evidence and validity domain.

03 · DECISION

What should engineering do?

Mathematics and computation are valuable only when they support interpretation, prediction, intervention, restoration, design or control under explicit constraints.

Research method

Physics constrains the abstraction.

The mathematical object is chosen because it matches a physical or operational mechanism—not because it is sophisticated. The resulting model is then analyzed, computed, tested against uncertainty and evidence, and used only within a stated validity domain.

\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{phys}} \rightarrow \mathcal C \rightarrow (\mathcal G,\mathfrak I) \rightarrow F_{\mathcal G} \rightarrow \mathcal V \rightarrow \rho_g \rightarrow u^\star\)

Current research focus

Interdependent Power–Transportation Systems

The primary physical application is the two-way dependency between electrical infrastructure and mobility systems, with information and organization included when they materially alter operation, coordination or recovery.

Physical system

Power ↔ Transportation

Power enables charging, signaling and electrically dependent mobility; transportation changes spatial charging demand, accessibility, restoration logistics and the location of mobile energy assets. The research problem is to identify and formalize those mechanisms rather than collapse them into a generic network edge.

Current scientific questions

Structure. Which cross-sector dependencies are physically real and operationally consequential?

Dynamics. How do disturbances propagate across interfaces and time scales?

Viability. Which system trajectories remain inside acceptable service, safety and sustainability constraints?

Geometry. When justified, can state-space metrics quantify distance to critical boundaries?

Intervention. Which control, restoration or design decision improves coupled-system outcomes?

Scientific standard

Evidence before strong claims.

Definition Model Computation Verification Validation

These are related but not interchangeable. A mathematically elegant model can still be physically inadequate; a reproducible numerical result can still be empirically unvalidated.

  • mechanism before model;
  • physics before abstraction;
  • uncertainty when it changes the engineering conclusion;
  • verification separated from empirical validation;
  • explicit validity domains and limitations.
